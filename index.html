1 Specialists need a supervisor that can refuse
Technical foundation

LungChat's 1,080 ablation tests showed a supervisor-plus-specialist structure cut token use in half and improved refusal of out-of-scope questions by 35 percentage points over a flat single-agent design. A flat agent has no natural boundary to say none of its specialists cover a question.

Chapter 1: Multi-agent orchestration

2 Ground answers in tools, not memorized weights
Technical foundation

TxAgent reasons over 211 curated tools instead of storing facts in its weights, reaching 92.1 percent accuracy across 3,168 drug-reasoning tasks, beating models that answer from parameters alone. Retrieval is checkable; a memorized weight is not.

Chapter 4: The closing vision

3 Triangulate before asserting a claim
Technical foundation

DrugAgent only composes a claim once three independent agents, an ML predictor, a knowledge-graph reasoner, and a retrieval-augmented literature search, agree, reaching 98.8 percent faithfulness. A manual check of 3,000-plus citations in highly cited papers found close to 39 percent did not actually support the claim attached to them.

Chapter 2: Grounding the agent

4 Fit the representation to the question
Technical foundation

A general embedding is the right tool when the question is broad and evidence is abundant. A graph-aligned one earns its cost when data is sparse, which is exactly where CLEAR improved F1 by up to 30 percent and where GATSBI's advantage widened for understudied proteins.

Chapters 8 and 9: Embeddings and graph prediction

5 Name the hidden assumption before you trust a prediction
Technical foundation

Four single-sample network methods reported different accuracy numbers until Kimberly Glass recast them in one notation and found their scale factor buys accuracy by sacrificing specificity, a trade-off invisible in the headline score alone. A method is only as trustworthy as the assumption it hides.

Chapter 10: The limits of network prediction

6 Ontologies anchor what a model can't promise
Organizational approach

A language model's fluent answer about a cell type can drift with training data and phrasing. A curated ontology is a fixed, agreed reference point a probabilistic model cannot guarantee on its own, the closing argument of the conference: AI and ontologies are partners, not rivals.

Chapter 5: Ontologies and schemas

7 Provenance travels with the fact, not beside it
Organizational approach

The Cell Knowledge Network stores a confidence measure and source directly on each graph edge rather than in a separate table, and FAIRSCAPE packages the same discipline as RO-Crates scored against 28 AI-readiness criteria. A fact's origin has to be inseparable from the fact itself.

Chapter 6: Registries, provenance, and quality control

8 Structure at ingest, not after the fact
Organizational approach

MetaHQ harmonizes hand-curated annotations from 13 sources into a database covering nearly 200,000 samples, and Canopy enforces structured metadata with CEDAR templates from the moment a dataset is submitted. Unstructured free text at scale becomes noisy input for everything downstream.

Chapter 11: FAIR data infrastructure

9 Benchmarks must resist gaming
Organizational approach

MedHopQA hides 1,000 scored questions inside a 10,000-question pool with withheld answers so a model cannot have memorized the key, while BAISBench grades AI-scientist systems against a floor of six graduate bioinformaticians rather than the model's own confidence.

Chapter 3: Proving it works

10 Consent and population are computable fields
People and governance

CTRL records a participant's consent choices in a standardized, machine-actionable form so a system can programmatically check whether a specific reuse is covered. Majumder's population-history keynote makes the same point from the other direction: the population a claim was measured in is part of the claim, not a footnote.

Chapters 12 and 15: Consent, licensing, and the keynote arc

11 AI drafts, a human or a cheap check decides
People and governance

PosterSentry's 10-kilobyte classifier screens every poster at 87.3 percent accuracy before anything expensive runs on it, and the biocuration competency framework names AI literacy as a curator skill, not a replacement for curation.

Chapter 13: AI in the curation pipeline

12 A public answer outlives a private one
People and governance

A private chatbot exchange is not searchable, not peer-reviewed, and not reusable by the next person who asks the same question, the exact things a public forum thread used to provide for free. Optimizing for one person's fast answer trades away the next person's shortcut.

Chapter 14: The closing culture debate

13 Risk lives in the connections, not the components
People and governance

A generative model and a similarity-based screening tool can each work perfectly and still combine to produce a sequence that evades screening, because neither component failed, the connection between them did. Capital moves toward what's fundable faster than governance moves toward what's safe.
